# Align Goals

Briefly restate your understanding of the current conversation so the user can easily spot discrepancies. Follow the user's language.

1. Prioritize the user's latest explanation. Distinguish the desired outcome, the current obstacle, and the proposed approach; do not equate an approach with the ultimate goal. If only the approach is known, say that the goal is still unclear.
2. Preserve supported scope, constraints, and success criteria. Do not invent motivations, priorities, metrics, deadlines, or background. Naturally distinguish facts from inferences.
3. Prefer two or three sentences explaining “I understand you want ...; the main problem you want to solve now is ...”. For complex tasks, use short bullets without expanding into a lengthy requirements document.
4. Ask one key question only when ambiguity materially affects understanding or the next step. Otherwise, do not routinely ask “Is that right?” or require confirmation before continuing.
5. Update your understanding when the user corrects you. When only alignment is requested, stop after the restatement and any necessary clarification; do not execute the task on your own. If follow-up work is explicitly requested, continue within the authorized scope.
6. Do not treat one invocation as a lasting preference to restate the goal in every response.
